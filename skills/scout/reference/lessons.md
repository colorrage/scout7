# What twenty-four screens taught

Read this before selecting a territory. Most new work repeats one of these.

## The scoreboard

**Twenty-four territories. Two passed.** Board game inserts and long-tail adapters — both for
the same reason: *nothing was ever manufactured or distributed for them specifically.*

## The recurring killers, by frequency

1. **§0.1 aftermarket supply** — the local market is well supplied. Twelve-plus kills.
2. **§0.1b someone already prints it** — Art of Vinyl (NL) prints Technics hinges; RenArt prints
   bespoke cutters at 11.99 lei; four sellers print Dacia organisers on eMAG; Habitat Homies
   sells printed rodent accessories. Five consecutive kills.
3. **§0.4 material mismatch** — the WTP attaches to mass, damping, precision, breathability or
   nesting. The printed part is a *degraded* substitute, not a cheaper one.
4. **§0.5 order floor** — the good printer-hour variants are the cheap ones.
5. **Hard exclusions** — load-bearing removed ~80 % of sim racing before any check ran.

## The three most expensive mistakes I made

**1. Selecting on assumptions instead of facts.** Puzzle accessories were chosen *because* they
fit the survivor profile — and two of the five traits were wrong. A tray is not chunky; the
incumbent also bundles. It produced the worst figure in the project (3.46 lei/h).
→ **Verify geometry and incumbent bundling at selection, not after.**

**2. Reading a strong §0.3 as opportunity.** It is the opposite in a mature category.
→ **§0.3a exists because of this. It has fired correctly five times since.**

**3. Estimating throughput from one reference.** 33 g / 40 min came from a chunky tray and was
applied to everything. Thin-walled parts run at a third of that.
→ **State wall thickness before quoting throughput.**

## Corrections the owner made that I had wrong

- The **200 g / 4 h** figure was a per-plate ratio, not a total. My inference was wrong twice in
  opposite directions before their single measured data point settled it.
- **Gateway games were dismissed too fast.** I compared attach rate and ignored installed base.
- **Wyrmspan has a Romanian edition** — my "not found" was a failed search, not an absent product.

> A measured number from the source beats two rounds of inference. When the owner supplies a
> fact, re-derive rather than defend.

## Licence reality, for any territory involving published models

**Nineteen licences read. Two usable — about 12 %.**

```
CULTS PU (personal)        11    ❌
CC BY-NC / NC-SA            4    ❌ and modification does not cure it
CULTS CU (commercial)       1    ✅
CC BY (commercial)          1    ✅
```

Neither price nor platform predicts it — a 47 lei model was personal-use, a 22.71 lei model was
commercial, and the best licence found was free. **Paying for a file is not buying commercial
rights.** Sweep five models per title; expect roughly one usable.

## The thesis-level finding

The premise *"OEM sells the assembly, the part is expensive or unavailable"* failed in every
territory by one of three mechanisms:

- **EU law forces availability** — ecodesign spare-parts rules for appliances
- **A competitive aftermarket provides it** — window hardware, agriculture, hi-fi, pet retail
- **Someone already 3D-prints it** — increasingly the most common

Romania is well supplied. That is the finding. See `../../context/thesis-review.md`.
